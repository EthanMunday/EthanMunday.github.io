Once again, the University of Portsmouth gave us the opportunity to showcase our game Curation, this time at EGX 2024 which for this year was merged with MCM Comic Con. Unlike the relatively small yet very enjoyable [Barclays Game Frenzy](/blog-posts/curation-at-barclays/), EGX is one of the biggest gaming conventions in the UK and, since it's being merged with MCM Comic Con, I was expecting a bit more of a crowd than last time.

Being such a big event, we had to book our tickets in advance. Since this time the game was being directly shown by the University of Portsmouth, none of us had any obligation to attend, yet I couldn't pass up this opportunity whilst making this also my first gaming convention. Funnily enough, my younger brother had a college trip to go there on the same day however decided to skip that and book alongside me just to get out of the college's hands.

I did not know what to expect when arriving. The venue for this event was ExCeL (funny capitilisation included) in London. When arriving, we did not have priority passes so we had to walk the entire lengths down to registration. Along the way, we noticed 2 things. Firstly, the convention centre was huge. We were walking for a good 5 minutes from front to back and it looked like a tunnel with no exit. Secondly, there were hundreds, possibly close to a thousand or more people who were attending, many of them dressed up in cosplay ranging from simple to simply insane. From when we got into the waiting hall while waiting for the event to open, I knew it would definitely leave a strong impression on me.

The first thing I noticed is that due to the merge, roughly 70% of the event was Comic Con while only 30% was EGX. Though I did very much enjoy the selection of EGX attendants, I do wish there was a bigger game section, or perhaps I came on the wrong day and the later days. There were a lot of bought out stalls selling many different things, mostly comic related. Many sorts of tabletop media, illustrations and written media around popular comic or anime characters, and mystery bags which did make me ponder why people would feel the need to buy them whilst also not having the irony lost on me that this is just one of the many instances of a lottery style random rewards system. Part of me was also tempted to spend some of the money that I brought however I saw no purpose for overpaying at any of these stalls. We went over to the University of Portsmouth stall and saw our game up and running, albeit poorly due to the weird setup they had. Many other universities and indie games were shown off here, including some that I played at Barclays. Apparently quite a few had won funding through the [Tranzfuser Games Fund](https://tranzfuser.com/) which I was very happy to see. Their projects also have clearly been improved from the versions we saw at Barclays and were great to try out. There were even a few projects that we recognised from the TIGA awards which I could finally try out and were just as entertaining. 

In terms of major titles, unfortunately there was not much that interested me. Apparently their was meant to be a showing of Call of Duty Black Ops 6 however it seemed to be absent the day I was there. The main booth I saw was a Bandai Namco booth, combined with their merchandise store which I was not surprised to see given I've been to the actual store in London before. At their booth were playable demos for Little Nightmares 3, Dragon Ball: Sparkling Zero and Unknown 9: Awakening. There was also the main stage featuring tournaments for different games, a LAN area equiped with many games to play with others and an intersting arcade/retro area with many older games to try out including a Dance Dance Revolution machine, a rare sight which I didn't think existed in the UK at this point.

Overall, it was quite an enjoyable event. I do think I preferred the Barclays Game Frenzy due to it's calmer atmosphere and game focus however I would enjoy attending in the future, perhaps at a time when the two events aren't merge to give more space for a larger range of attractions to enjoy.
