Roughly 2 weeks ago now (yes, I took that long to get to writing this), I showed off our game Curation at GDLX 2025. It was a very long day but was great to meet back up with our team and discover new indie games being created by people like us around Southern England and beyond.

This venue was the University of the Creative Arts Farnham. I had never been to Farnham before and I enjoyed it's rural atmosphere and small town vibe. The university itself was nice and not to unfamiliar to me compared to other unis and colleges I've visited in the past.

However on this day, some primordial force spoke a rule. The rule for the day was "public transport will do public transport things". The journey to the venue started with a cancelled train and a rail replacement bus which was not too impactful. This lasted for 2 and a half hours until I finally arrived in Farnham.

This time I was marked down as a contributor so I went inside the venue early where we had a nice introduction from the event holders before the event began. A lot more of my time was spent at the desk explaining and guiding people who wanted to play our game. There were a lot more people than expected and even more interested people who gave very helpful feedback so if you somehow managed to find my blog here, thank you. Of course I did also get a chance to try out some games from others. The main stand out to me was a game called [Oubliette](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3288890/Oubliette/), an action packed game about falling down the infinite sewer pits of Paris and shooting weird creatures, all with a classic detective theme. I loved the fast paced movement and quick decision making this game required and looked very promising.

After a 6 hour long event, I followed the immaginary conga line down to the after party where I got a nice amount of BBQ ribs to eat whilst talking with the team and also networking with some others who were at the event. It was slightly confusing however a nice way to relax and eat something after a long day. But as the rule goes, "public transport will be public transport". The trains arrived in the wrong order almost causing a switcheroo between me and another programmer at Circle Back, the trains that did run had some sort of issues preventing certain stops from being available and the final Uber ride home took 20 minutes to arrive. Although it was a very exhausting day, it was a great event and I thoroughly enjoyed my day.

Finally, since this was delayed so long, I can announce that I'll be returning to Barclays Game Frenzy and showcasing the newest build of Curation. I'm excited to return and see new and old faces.
